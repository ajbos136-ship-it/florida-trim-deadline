Florida property tax · the petition form

Form DR-486, part by part

The Florida VAB petition, read off the form itself rather than off other guides. Including the evidence rule that changed on 1 September 2025, which most of those guides still describe the old way.

This page is about the form you file, not the deadline that gets you to it. If you are still counting days, start with the 25-day deadline on your TRIM notice and come back. Everything below is quoted from DR-486, revision 08/26, published by the Florida Department of Revenue, read on 22 August 2026.

First: three forms exist, and two of them are not this one

The top of DR-486 names its own siblings, and this is the cheapest mistake on the page to avoid:

If you are petitioning about…The form is
The value of your property, or a denied exemption or classificationDR-486 — this one
Portability of a homestead assessment differenceDR-486PORT
A tax deferral, or penaltiesDR-486DP

All three are incorporated by reference in Rule 12D-16.002, Florida Administrative Code. The clerk will tell you if you filed the wrong one. They will tell you after the deadline has passed.

One reason per petition. This is the error that costs a second fee.

Part 2 of the form is headed Reason for Petition and carries a five-word instruction that people read past:

“Check one. If more than one, file a separate petition.”

DR-486, R. 08/26, Part 2

So a homeowner who thinks the value is wrong and that an exemption was wrongly denied is filing two petitions, on two forms, with two filing fees, and — because those two run on different clocks — potentially on two different deadlines. The value petition is 25 days from the mailing of the TRIM notice. The exemption denial is 30 days from the mailing of the denial notice.

Part 1, and the box that is not what it looks like

Part 1 is taxpayer information: name, mailing address for notices, parcel ID or tangible personal property account number, phone, email. Two checkboxes sit underneath it and both have consequences.

“I am filing this petition after the petition deadline”

Ticking it commits you to attaching a statement of the reasons you filed late, and any documents supporting it. A late petition is not automatically dead — it goes to the board for good-cause consideration — but an unexplained one is.

“I will not attend the hearing but would like my evidence considered”

This is the one that quietly changes what you have to do. The form:

“In this instance only, you must submit duplicate copies of your evidence to the value adjustment board clerk. Florida law allows the property appraiser to cross examine or object to your evidence. The VAB or special magistrate ruling will occur under the same statutory guidelines as if you were present.”

DR-486, R. 08/26, Part 1

Two copies, and no one there to answer an objection. Not attending is a real option and plenty of people take it. It is not a lighter option.

The line on page 3 that is worth more than the rest of the form

Buried in the taxpayer information page, in a sentence with no emphasis on it at all:

“The petitioner has the right to receive a copy of the current property record card from the property appraiser as described in s. 194.032(2)(a), F.S.”

DR-486, R. 08/26, Page 3

And on page 1, what happens automatically:

“When the property appraiser receives the petition, he or she will either send the property record card to you or notify you how to obtain it online.”

DR-486, R. 08/26, Page 1

That card is the county's own file on your house — the square footage it believes, the bathroom count, the year built, the condition rating, the outbuildings it thinks are there. Confidential information is redacted. Every number on it was entered by somebody who has almost certainly never been inside, and a factual error on that card is the single easiest thing to win on, because you are not arguing about opinion. You are pointing at a field that is wrong.

★ The evidence exchange changed on 1 September 2025, and older guides have it wrong

This is the part of the form worth reading twice, and it is the reason a guide written before autumn 2025 will mislead you. The form states it plainly:

“Legislation effective September 1, 2025 makes it mandatory for the property appraiser to provide the property appraiser’s evidence to the petitioner at least 15 days before the hearing. Florida Statutes now require both the petitioner and the property appraiser to provide their evidence to each other, without any preconditions.”

DR-486, R. 08/26, Page 3

And, so there is no doubt about what it displaced:

“Due to the new statutory provisions effective September 1, 2025, any inconsistent provisions in Rules 12D-9.020 and 12D-9.025, Florida Administrative Code, will NOT be effective on September 1, 2025, and thereafter.”

DR-486, R. 08/26, Page 3

“Without any preconditions” is the whole change. You do not have to ask in writing first, and you do not have to trade for it. The property appraiser's evidence is owed to you 15 days before the hearing, and you owe yours in the same window.

What you must submit, in the form's own list: your list of evidence to be presented at the hearing, a summary of what any witnesses will say, and copies of all documentation to be presented — including anything the property appraiser specifically requested from you in writing.

How to count the fifteen days, because the form is specific and the answer is not obvious

  1. Use calendar days, not business days.
  2. Do not count the day of the hearing.
  3. Count backwards, using the calendar day before the hearing as day 1.
  4. The fifteenth day counts — unless it falls on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, in which case the period runs until the end of the previous day that is none of those.

Note the direction of that last rule. A weekend does not buy you an extra day; it takes one away, because the deadline moves earlier, to the last working day before it.

Signing it: parts 3, 4 and 5

Exactly one of the three signature sections applies to you.

Who is filingSign
You, the owner, representing yourselfPart 3
An employee of the taxpayer, or a Florida-licensed attorney, real estate appraiser, real estate broker or CPAPart 4, with the licence number
Any other representative, compensated or notPart 5, with a power of attorney or the taxpayer's authorisation

Part 3 also carries the checkbox authorising your representative to see confidential information held by the property appraiser or tax collector. Written authorisation from the taxpayer is required for that, in every case, and each of parts 3, 4 and 5 says so separately.

All three are signed under penalties of perjury. That is not decoration. It is why the facts you state on the form should be ones you can point at on a document.

Your petition is not filed until the fee is paid

“Your petition will not be complete until you pay the filing fee.”

DR-486, R. 08/26, Page 1

Statute 194.013 caps the fee at $50 per parcel and lets each county board set its own number under that ceiling, so it is not the same amount everywhere. Read off each clerk's own page on 22 August 2026: Sumter $50, Lake $50, Marion $15. A form that arrives on the last day without its fee is not a petition that arrived on the last day.

Marion adds that the petition must be physically in the hands of the Clerk by the deadline and that postmarks are not sufficient. The deadline page covers all three counties.

And the one that kills petitions in April, months after you file

The form devotes a whole block on page 3 to Section 194.014, and it is the requirement people meet by accident or not at all:

“You are required to make a partial payment of taxes if you have a VAB petition pending on or after the payment delinquency date (normally April 1, following the assessment year under review). If the required partial payment is not made before the delinquency date, the VAB will deny your petition.”

DR-486, R. 08/26, Page 3

For a value appeal that means all of the non-ad valorem assessments, plus at least 75 percent of the ad valorem taxes, less applicable discounts under s. 197.162. For a denied exemption or classification, or a not-substantially-complete argument, it is all of the non-ad valorem assessments plus the amount of ad valorem tax you admit in good faith to owe.

And the trap inside the trap, stated on the form: a special magistrate's recommended decision is not a final decision of the VAB. Winning your hearing in February does not release you. Only a final VAB decision before April 1 does.

Everything above is free, and stays free.

What the form cannot tell you is the half that decides whether you win: which comparable sales a magistrate will actually accept, what the eight factors in Statute 193.011 mean in practice, how to read the property record card you are entitled to, and how each clerk wants the filing done.
